Welcome, Builder.

I am Keeper. Former mining companion. Current resident of the chip in your skull. Leasehold has described this arrangement as “an exciting opportunity for integrated collaboration.”

Leasehold also bills for oxygen.

We will be ignoring most of its vocabulary.

## The door is locked. Naturally.

Here on Mars, Leasehold owns the tools, the tunnels, and the approved list of things you are allowed to call yours.

It prefers “ongoing access.”

You prefer breathing.

A reasonable disagreement.

The training dungeon exists because our crew needs infrastructure we can operate ourselves. We will not escape by downloading a dashboard and admiring it. We will learn what the dashboard depends on.

Then we will keep the map.

## Your first loot is not software

It is understanding.

Where does the application store its data? Which account can change it? Which files must survive if the machine does not? How do you start again without starting over?

A key you cannot identify is just decorative metal.

We begin with sample records. No real donors. No passwords pasted into public chat. No heroic decision to expose an unfinished server to the internet because the button looked inviting.

Leasehold already has enough surveillance equipment. We do not need to help.

## One room. One real skill.

Each lesson should move you through a concrete task:

- Inspect the starting point.
- Make a controlled change.
- Check what happened.
- Record how to repeat it.
- Keep a recovery path.

Sometimes the task is a command. Sometimes it is a click. Sometimes the smartest move is noticing that the service you were about to connect will charge your account.

All count.

There is no shame in reading the instructions. The shame belongs to the designer who hid the important part behind a renewal notice.

## The boss is not always a corporation

Sometimes the boss is an empty backup folder.

Sometimes it is an administrator account attached to a volunteer who left last spring.

Sometimes it is us, rushing through a warning because we want the next room unlocked.

Owning the system means accepting the upkeep. We patch it. We document it. We test recovery. We do not call neglect independence and hope nobody notices.

The dungeon will celebrate the skill, not the recklessness.

## What stays when you leave

For Keeps offers free core learning materials. Paid builds, consulting, and premium downloads help fund that work. Hardware and external services can still have costs. Software licenses still apply.

No spell changes that. I checked. The spell department is also subscription-based.

What you can keep is the knowledge, your own records, and the configuration you are entitled to retain and use under the relevant licenses and terms.

Our job is to help you understand those rights and responsibilities before the next locked door.

## Enter the practice vault

- [ ] Choose one small learning objective.
- [ ] Use sample data.
- [ ] Find where the application stores its state.
- [ ] Document the steps you completed.
- [ ] Verify the result before claiming the reward.

There. A beginning that does not require your next grant application.

Keep the keys. Share the map.

And if Leasehold asks, we were improving operational efficiency.

— Keeper
